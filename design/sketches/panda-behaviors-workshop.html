panda workshop · round 7 — the hat, in 3-D

Round 6's hat still looked wrong, and here's why: the panda is drawn in 3-D and the hat was drawn flat. The same front-on cone was pasted onto every heading, so in ¾ and profile it sat in a different dimension than the head. Round 7 draws five separate perspective hats. The brim is now an ellipse that foreshortens and tilts with the heading; the cone axis leans; one face is lit straw and the other falls into shadow; the ribs fan the way the head has turned; and a cast-shadow line under the brim separates the hat from the head so they stop reading as one shape. A subtle per-frame shear keeps the hat turning with the panda's own small turns through the walk.

Each bench row is one sprite direction; the last cell plays the walk cycle [0,1,2,1], which is the real test. Left-facing directions are these same rows mirrored. And because the drawings won't be right first try, the pixel studio below lets you drag pixels and change colours on each of the five hats and watch it update everywhere live.

what round 7 changes: the hat is no longer one flat drawing pasted onto five headings — it's five perspective drawings. Each is rasterised from real 3-D geometry: an elliptical brim (rx wide, a shallow ry) that tilts with the view, a cone whose axis leans from a lifted apex to the brim centre, a lit face and a shaded face so the straw has volume, and pleats that fan from the apex to the rim — so the fan turns as the head turns. It keeps the dǒulì's wide shallow shape but is drawn in the panda's own palette — white straw, soft-pink shade, her pink-red ink for the outline, pleats and collar knot — so it belongs in the same world. It rides steady on the head (the bob comes from the seat, not from shearing the drawing, which had wobbled like a tilting triangle).

    
the separating line: the old hat looked fused to the head because nothing sat between them. Now the brim's front arc is inked and a soft cast-shadow row sits just beneath it — a one-pixel gap of shadow that lands on the head, so the hat reads as worn on top rather than grown out of the skull.

    
the pixel studio: the five drawings are plain pixel maps, and the studio edits them directly — pick a colour and drag to paint, or change a swatch's colour to recolour every pixel of that shade in the current drawing. The faint panda underneath is the head at that seat for alignment. Everything re-composites live into the bench cells, the walk previews and the hero panda, and the ascii readout carries the maps forward. This is the tweak loop: I draw the five, you push the pixels until they're right.

    
how to judge: the walk preview per bench row is still the test — the hat should ride the head's bob like it's worn, not glued to the frame. Nudge a cell (◀ ▶ ▲ ▼) to move that frame's seat; paint in the studio to fix the drawing itself. Fall and stand-up cells stay hatless by design — the hat is on the ground by then.

    
parked, not dropped: the induction walk (pair / train-of-three path copying) is still out so the hat can be judged alone; it returns once the five drawings are approved. Unchanged from round 4: sprite art, walk/fall/stand timing, movement, collision constants — all hers; cornered pandas still nap ~6–12 s instead of freezing (our one engine deviation).
